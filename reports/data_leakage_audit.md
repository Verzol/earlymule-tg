# Data leakage audit

Dataset `80K-earlymule-mule`, code at commit `9d2d4d0` (72 point-in-time features), checked 2026-11-13. One row per item of the research plan's leakage audit (section 22). Each row says how the risk is closed, what shows it, and what is left. Column classes and their reasons are in `reports/column_classification.md`; the label rule in `reports/ground_truth_check.md`.

Time convention throughout: day index from the simulation start, end-of-day cut-off. A score for day t may use transfers dated on or before t.

## Simulator artefacts that are not leaks

These do not pass future or label information, but a model could use them as shortcuts. They are handled by the choice of population, not by the feature code.

- **Activity level.** AMLSim draws case members from accounts with regular normal activity, so activity alone separates mules from all accounts. The main population is regular accounts (at least 20 transfers up to day t), where every single screened feature stays between 0.45 and 0.57 ROC-AUC (`reports/ground_truth_check.md`, `python -m earlymule.data.shortcut_audit`).
- **Opening deposit.** The balance boost of the fork raises the deposit of active accounts, so `initial_deposit` scores 0.64 ROC-AUC over all accounts but 0.50 among regular accounts (same report).
- **Account ID.** Low IDs are more active (ROC-AUC 0.64); the ID is a key and rejected as a feature (row 9).
- **New-partner signal.** Normal accounts pay fixed partners on fixed schedules, so new-partner features are stronger than they would be in a bank (Data Spec section 9). This makes the task easier; it is a limitation of the data, recorded in the thesis.
